# Hit Feel, Editing, and Sound Layers

## Contact before emphasis

A readable strike follows preparation/intent → approach → encounter → reaction → recovery or next move. Specify striking surface, contact target, and force destination: feet/support and hips/shoulders drive the weapon; the recipient deforms, yields, slides, or rebounds; the environment carries follow-through. Misses, grazes, blocks, and hits receive different reactions/sounds. If contact fails, fix path/distance first.

Hit-feel card: `shot/event ID | attack line and receiving surface | principal key poses | encounter time | impact/yielding mechanism | displacement and secondary motion | material response | editing accent | sound event | effect intensity | recovery result`.

## Organizing accents

Acceleration and brief emphasis can create impact; constant speed can also let one clean action work. Pose changes, sound attacks, a local light core, brief holds, or short shakes can carry emphasis. Select by style rather than maximizing every device. Frame counts follow frame rate and dynamic previs; a universal three-frame shake formula is prohibited.

Heavy hits retain wind-up and stopping costs. Differentiate combinations through high/low lines, entry angles, rhythm, and consequences rather than identical sounds/back-leans for every punch. An unmoving strong defender can redirect force into the ground, shield rim, or attacker. For a strike that misses because the defender evades, use pass-by and speed cues, not a shattering hit sound.

## Separate picture and sound layers

Picture: primary action/deformation, weapon contact, material response, local ability, environmental aftermath, camera treatment. Validate the first two before adding layers. Editing can cut before contact, at contact, or on result; preserve the attack line. Bright light/whip blur cannot swallow all contact and consequences.

Sound stems: dialogue/breath, exertion/air movement, contact attack, bodily/mechanical response, material destruction, ambience, music, tails. Bind timing through event IDs rather than permanently flattening stems. Tails/ambience can cross cuts, with level/direction changing by spatial relationship. Choose layers by material/result; not every punch needs the same low-frequency explosion.

Plan audible foreground/background hierarchy and leave room for dialogue, important transients, and bass. Final listening checks clipping, masking, synchronization, and fatigue. Verify loudness targets, peaks, and delivery tracks against current release requirements; do not invent universal platform numbers. Waveform/level checks cannot replace listening.

## Editing and rhythm acceptance

Watch action without effects, then the full version. Check whether silent viewing explains the exchange, and listen for mismatched contact, breathing, or ambience. Temporary muting is diagnostic, not permission to skip actual listening. After a peak, retain landing, braking, breathing, or environmental residue so viewers can see where the hit landed and what changed.

Establish a combination's signature through one complete presentation, then develop through compression, variation, or a counter. Music beats must not remove defense mechanisms, force production, or critical consequences. See [character performance](character-and-performance.md) for dialogue/lip-sync handoff.

## Local contact sound versus overall sound

This file focuses on sound/picture relationships of contact, yielding, and recovery. [Overall sound](../sound/sound-and-voice.md) arranges environment, foley, music, and silence. Sound-effect analysis is not actual listening.

## All-style impact context reasoning

By default, silently reason through “struck part → force direction/strength → current support/protection → body/machine structure → environmental material.” Do not memorize a fixed “retch, turn head, lean back” reaction vocabulary. Make reaction, yielding, and recovery follow contact, distinguishing miss/block/hit. The same move should produce different outcomes under different states/materials.

- **Dynamic impact response:** light touch may change local tension or interrupt existing breathing; a heavy hit may carry an established stumble/slide/torso fold along its force. These are conditional candidates, not a one-to-one force/symptom table. For wall contact, inspect wall, armor, and force connections first. Crack/rebound only if material/force supports it and the original event needs it; not every hit bends a waist, causes blood, or shatters scenery.
- **Visual held-frame reasoning:** inspect existing sweat, rain, debris, or sparks and select very few local particles as speed references at contact. Exaggerated animation may briefly emphasize a key pose within the established style, depicted as a short arrest and particle travel. Realist footage does not treat long-suspended raindrops as real physics. Exact frame holds, retiming, and compositing remain production notes; prompts cannot promise frame-precise holds.
- **Material/effect coupling:** local sparks, dents, edge deformation, or pressure waves originate at actual contact and established power sources. Skip sparks when material does not support them; soft materials do not automatically emit metallic welding sparks. Force/displacement remain readable with effects off. Do not flash first and add contact afterward, or add a second burst to conceal failure.
- **Physiological sound:** choose one fitting exertion exhale, short shout, impact grunt, or recovery breath from stamina, injury, mouth/breathing structure, and habits. Do not loop a roar on every punch. Non-breathing machines use established mechanical feedback; restrained/silent characters retain their design. Brief audible content from existing sources may enter prose; label the actual sound execution route and unverified capabilities through [generation handoff](generation-handoff.md). Sound-effects support does not imply precise lip sync.

These refine consequences/presentation of established contact, not automatically add core mandatory actions. Noncritical particles are Tier C texture, limited by coherence, visibility and prompt length rather than a fixed number of atmosphere slots. Independent state changes such as falls, incapacitation, wall cracks, or weapon damage cannot enter for free: preserve the original situation and reject that candidate by default. If explicitly required, record the consequential event in the Tier A capacity check and carry its seam state. “All-style” does not mean all methods at once. Choose the most useful response for one encounter, dropping camera flourishes if necessary.
