# Situation Profiles: physicality, timing, and camera by context

v3.0 treated every conversation as two people at a table. That is one situation among many. A street argument, a farewell, a whispered threat, and a bedside talk differ in **movement, distance, hand business, voice, silence, overlap, camera, and light**. Choose a profile from the Context Profile (`core/context-routing.md`), then use `conversation-units.md` for turn anatomy and `lens/body-and-status.md` for the status vocabulary.

The tea scene in the examples is **profile A below**, and nothing else.

All thirteen profiles route through [spatial blocking](spatial-blocking.md) before fine gestures and coverage. Their movement tendencies are not a completed plan: choose maintain, relocate or mixed for each conversation unit, with a motive, the other person's response and a resulting geometry. Minimal motion is valid when grounded in the scene; pacing and changing anchors are not compulsory. Silent exchanges use the same spatial decision check.

## 1. The profiles

Each profile lists the starting parameters. They are tendencies to adapt, not rules.

**A. Seated formal (desk, tea table, council).** *Movement:* minimal; posture and prop business carry status. *Distance:* social band, furniture mediates. *Hands:* cups, pens, papers. *Voice:* controlled, measured. *Silence:* 20–35%. *Overlap:* rare. *Camera:* locked or slow push; two-shot, singles, inserts. *Light:* practical, directional. *Pitfall:* statues, with nobody moving.

**B. Standing confrontation (private).** *Movement:* small steps in and out of the personal band; weight shifts; pacing for the one who owns the room. *Hands:* clenched, pointing, or deliberately still. *Voice:* clipped; volume changes carry the escalation. *Silence:* 20–30%, with held beats before a hard line. *Overlap:* occasional. *Camera:* medium two-shot with a handheld push-in on the turn. *Pitfall:* both people planted like seated people but standing.

**C. Street argument (public, high arousal).** *Movement:* proximity violation, advance and retreat, big gestures that turn into grabs or shoves; onlookers drift into frame. *Hands:* large, fast, pointed. *Voice:* loud, fast, bursts of 1–4 words, **frequent overlap and interruption**, cracks at peak volume. *Silence:* 5–15%. *Camera:* handheld, participant, 28–35 mm, cutting to bystander reactions. *Light:* ambient daylight or street light. *Face:* public display rules apply to the audience, not to the opponent (`lens/face-pass.md` section 10). *Pitfall:* tidy turn-taking.

**D. Walking and talking.** *Movement:* the walk is the scene. Whoever leads sets pace; slowing or stopping is a status move; speeding up is an escape. *Alignment:* side by side (parity), one slightly ahead (leader and follower), one blocking (confrontation), walking away mid-sentence (rejection). *Hands:* tied to the walk (pockets, sleeve ends, carrying something). *Voice:* shorter breath groups, interrupted by obstacles. *Silence:* 20–40%, filled by footsteps. *Camera:* tracking beside, in front (facing both), or behind; handheld or Steadicam. *Pitfall:* the walk is a conveyor belt behind two talking heads.

**E. Farewell (platform, doorway, gate, dock, hospital door).** *Movement:* delay is the physical language: hands that will not release, a step toward and a step back, a straightening of a collar, an object handed over, a last look from the departing back. *Distance:* closes by half-steps and then hesitates. *Voice:* low, halting, sentences that do not finish, the real subject avoided. *Silence:* **35–55%.** *Overlap:* both starting to speak and yielding. *Camera:* longer lens (85–135 mm) compressing the pair, lingering holds, a **hold after the departure** (the empty space they leave). *Light:* soft, low, motivated by the place (platform lamps, dawn). *Pitfall:* an articulate speech instead of a failed one.

**F. Confession or intimate talk (private, low light).** *Movement:* very little; closeness or deliberate distance. *Hands:* hidden or reaching. *Voice:* soft, slow, long pauses before the hard line. *Silence:* 35–50%. *Face:* private display rules (the mask is lowered and leaks are larger). *Camera:* close, steady, shallow depth of field. *Light:* a single practical, deep shadow. *Pitfall:* a monologue delivered at the camera.

**G. Interrogation or squeeze.** *Movement:* the interrogator circles or looms; the subject is contained. *Props:* a file, a lamp, a cup left untouched. *Voice:* the interrogator is slow and even; the subject over-explains or goes silent. *Silence:* 30–45%, used as a weapon. *Camera:* low angle on the interrogator, a tight close-up on the subject at the crack. *Pitfall:* shouting as the only intensity.

**H. Public address, ceremony, or court.** *Movement:* formal; stillness in the speaker, murmuring audience. *Voice:* projected, structured, rhythmic. *Silence:* 10–20%, with an audience reaction as the pause. *Overlap:* a heckle breaks the form. *Camera:* wide to establish the audience, then the speaker, with reaction shots. *Face:* the public mask is strong, and leaks appear on the margins of the frame (a hand, a jaw). *Pitfall:* a conversational tone on a stage.

**I. Whispered exchange under threat (secret).** *Movement:* heads close, bodies turned away from the danger, eyes on the doorway. *Voice:* whisper, breath-heavy, 1.5–2.5 words/s, broken by listening. *Silence:* 40–55%, with noise as the tension. *Camera:* tight, low-key, a long lens, the threat out of focus in the background. *Pitfall:* a clear, projected whisper.

**J. Confined-space argument (car, kitchen, elevator).** *Movement:* constrained by the actual space and circumstances; verify whether and why leaving is restricted. In a car seat or crowded elevator, avoidance may use gaze, torso orientation and reach; a kitchen may still permit routes around a counter. *Hands:* gripping a wheel, a counter, a bag. *Voice:* tight and loud for the room; the space makes volume feel larger. *Silence:* 15–30%. *Camera:* handheld, close, a cramped frame, reflections. *Pitfall:* people with room to pace.

**K. Bedside or grief.** *Movement:* near stillness; a hand held, a cloth, a bowl. *Voice:* halting, 1–2 words/s, unfinished sentences, breath. *Silence:* 40–60%. *Camera:* low, steady, long holds, a watchful distance. *Light:* soft, a lamp or window. *Pitfall:* over-articulate grief.

**L. Post-exertion exchange (after a fight or a run).** *Movement:* hands on knees, leaning on walls, weapons lowered but held. *Voice:* breathless, fragments, 1–2 words/s with gaps for breath. *Silence:* 30–45%, filled with breathing. *Body:* sweat, dust, a limp, shaking hands. *Camera:* observer with a slight hold, handheld breath. *Pitfall:* complete, calm sentences from a character who just fought.

**M. Banter and social ease (a meal, a walk, a shop).** *Movement:* relaxed, expansive, touching objects. *Voice:* quick, overlapping, laughter. *Silence:* 5–15%. *Camera:* loose, mobile, medium shots; two-shot with a drifting handheld. *Pitfall:* delivery of jokes in a stiff posture.

## 2. Speech timing by arousal

Planning figures only (read it aloud for the real number). They replace the single pace in the earlier table.

| State | English words per second | Chinese characters per second | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calm | 2–3 | 3.5–5 | Long breath groups |
| Hot anger | 3–4 | 5–7 | Bursts, overlap, clipped words |
| Cold anger | 1.5–2.5 | 3–4 | Slow, precise, long pauses before the sharp word |
| Whisper | 1.5–2.5 | 2.5–4 | Breath-heavy, needs room |
| Grief | 1–2 | 2–3.5 | Halting, unfinished |
| Breathless | 1–2 | 2–3 | Fragments with breath gaps |
| Panic or shouting | 3–4.5 | 5–8 | Short words, cracks, repeats |

Fit the line length to the clip using the pace for the **character's state**, not a default.

## 3. Unit skeletons by situation

The five-stage skeleton in `conversation-units.md` (gambit, probe, evasion or pressure, turn, exit) is the **formal exchange** form. Others:

| Situation | Skeleton |
|---|---|
| Street argument | Accusation, counter, escalation (volume then proximity), the line that is crossed, a physical or social rupture, the aftermath beat |
| Farewell | Small talk to avoid it, the delay, the object or touch, the failed attempt at the real sentence, the departure, the hold on the empty space |
| Confession | Approach, a preface, the disclosure, the reaction, the silence |
| Interrogation | Setup, pressure, a false exit, the squeeze, the crack or the refusal |
| Negotiation | Positions, a trade offer, a bluff, a counter, the floor reached, the pen or the walk-out |
| Walking talk | Start, a topic that shifts the pace, a stop, resumption at a different alignment |
| Post-exertion | Breath, a bare fact, a flash of humor or pain, a decision |

## 4. Physical-state modifiers (apply to any profile)

| State | Effect on body and speech |
|---|---|
| Injured | Protects the limb, shifts weight, shorter breath groups, avoids the movement that hurts |
| Exhausted | Slack face, slower speech, support-seeking (leaning, sitting), slower reaction |
| Cold | Hunched shoulders, tucked hands, visible breath, a tightened jaw |
| Drunk | Looser posture, overshoot in gestures, slurred rhythm, a repeated point |
| Hurried | Standing in a half-turn toward the exit, checking the time or door, clipped sentences |
| Terrified | A narrow focus, trembling hands, a dry swallow, failures to complete sentences |

## 5. Public and private

Public settings change **voice and face before blocking**: volume drops and speech becomes formal in front of an authority; a smile or a flat professional face holds until a private moment. Private settings release the mask. Use the display rules in `lens/face-pass.md` section 10 and keep the distinction visible: the same two people in a courtroom corridor and in a closed car should look and sound different.

## 6. Procedure

1. Name the profile from the Context Profile.
2. Choose the skeleton, the pace for the characters' states, and the silence share.
3. Build the unit spatial spine using [spatial blocking](spatial-blocking.md): starting geometry, cue/goal, maintain/relocate/mixed, path or concrete hold reason, counterpart response, ending geometry and consequence. Do this before props; not once per line. A table, doorway or window is usable only if present, reachable and relevant.
4. Choose camera behavior and light from the profile.
5. Check against the pitfall and the spatial QA in `spatial-blocking.md` section 6. Do not accept either unmotivated pacing or an unexplained static default.
6. If the situation changes mid-scene (a quarrel becomes an embrace), re-route and write the change as a turn.
