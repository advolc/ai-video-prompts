# Context Routing Examples

Each case shows a **Context Profile**, the **routing record**, and what to avoid copying from the canyon duel or the tea scene. Two short prompts (Compact tier) show how different the writing becomes. None has been run on a video model.

## 1. Stealth takedown

**Profile:** Purpose: a decisive act, showing competence and menace | Power: assailant absolute | State: calm, breath controlled | Space: narrow inn corridor, night, one lamp | Audience: none | Tools: short blade, wrapped hands | Tone: realist thriller | Fail-state: noise or discovery.
**Routing:** Mode **stealth** (Tier A 0–1 per clip; approach and strike stay one unit); camera **observer**, long lens, a slow creep; sound near-silent with one controlled sound; look: one hard source, cool low saturation; end mode **hold** (aftermath). *Generation fail-state:* a flurry of blades where one contact was meant.
**Do not copy from the duel:** initiative changes, a clean block-and-reset rhythm, a held standoff.

> Live-action thriller, 16:9, long lens, shallow depth of field, cool night palette, fine grain, locked-off then a slow creep. A narrow inn corridor, one oil lamp guttering at the far end. Ren, thirties, lean, in dark close-fitting cloth with wrapped hands, moves along the wall on the outer edges of his feet, breath shallow, a short blade reversed along his forearm. Through a half-open door a heavy man sleeps on his back. Ren stops, waits through one slow breath, slides through the gap; his left hand covers the man's mouth as the blade finds the throat. The lamp flame leans once, then settles. Ren stays motionless, listening, the corridor silent.

## 2. Last stand

**Profile:** Purpose: protect those behind the door | Power: attackers dominant | State: wounded, spent by the second half | Space: a doorway, night, loose stone | Audience: none | Tools: short staff | Tone: realist | Fail-state: collapse.
**Routing:** Mode **last stand** (Tier A 2–4 with overlap; no clean resets; fatigue visible: guard sinks, steps shorten); camera **participant**, handheld at shoulder height; sound ragged breath and impact; look: harsh mixed light, coarse grain; end mode **handoff in motion** (stamina and injury carried in the seam).
**Do not copy from the duel:** a lull after each exchange; both fighters equally fresh at the end.

## 3. Sparring

**Profile:** Purpose: teaching, with warmth | Power: master leads, student learns | State: fresh, warming up | Space: a courtyard in morning | Audience: a younger student watching | Tools: wooden swords | Tone: gentle | Fail-state: a rush that could injure.
**Routing:** Mode **sparring** (Tier A 1–2; stops to correct are **legitimate**; light contact; a final touch ends it); camera **observer**, relaxed, closer on the correction; sound wooden clacks and a casual remark; look: soft morning light, clear palette; end mode **hold**.
**Do not copy from the duel:** lethal tone, blood, a standoff.

## 4. Street argument

**Profile:** Purpose: a quarrel that breaks something | Power: parity, shifting | State: agitated, voices rising | Space: a public lane with onlookers | Audience: bystanders watching | Relationship: former friends | Tone: realist | Fail-state: a public rupture.
**Routing:** Situation **C (street argument)**; movement: advance, retreat, gestures that become a shove; speech 3–4 words per second, **overlap**, silence 5–15%; camera **participant**, handheld 28–35 mm, cutting to bystander reactions; display rules: the audience suppresses expression until the rupture, then the mask fails; end mode **handoff in motion** or **cut on action**.
**Do not copy from the tea scene:** seated posture, prop business, long holds.

## 5. Farewell

**Profile:** Purpose: say goodbye without saying it | Power: parity, tender | State: calm on the surface, held feeling | Space: a country train platform, dawn | Audience: a few distant travelers | Relationship: intimate | Tone: restrained drama | Fail-state: leaving without having said it.
**Routing:** Situation **E (farewell)**; movement: delay (collar, scarf, half-steps); speech 1–2 words per second with unfinished lines; silence 35–55%; camera **observer**, 100 mm compression, a **hold after the departure**; look: soft dawn backlight, low contrast; end mode **hold**.
**Do not copy from the tea scene:** control through props and long silences used as a weapon.

> Live-action drama, 16:9, 100 mm lens, soft dawn backlight, low contrast, fine grain, locked-off. A country platform at dawn, breath showing. Mei, late twenties, hair pinned loosely, a wool coat buttoned one button off, hands clasped; Jun, thirty, canvas bag, worn scarf. Jun straightens her collar and lets his hand rest there a moment too long. "Take care of..." he says, and stops. "You'll miss it," she says, low. The whistle sounds. He presses the scarf into her hands, steps back twice, and looks once more from the carriage door. The train pulls out; the camera holds on Mei and the empty rail, her fingers closed around the scarf.

## 6. Public vs private face (same beat)

**Beat:** a character hears that someone has died.
- **Courtroom corridor (public):** strong mask, a composed face; the jaw tightens, the throat moves, one hand closes on a file; a fast recovery; a wider shot lets the body carry it.
- **Closed car (private):** the mask drops after the door shuts; the breath breaks, the hands shake on the wheel, the face crumples slowly; a close shot.

Write the public mask first, then the private after-beat; that contrast is the scene.
